# Blueprint Bench Lessons V0

Status date: 2026-08-08

These lessons came from the Blueprint Reading construction bench and should guide the next full-flow v0 test.

## Student Guide Design

- The study guide is student-facing, not internal documentation.
- The cover should be a branded series cover, not a decorative image cover.
- The cover should emphasize the course title first, then lesson title, lesson number, level, and a short relevant quote or provocative line when available.
- Audience, prerequisites, unit policy, and internal production notes do not belong on the cover.
- The BuildStak icon should be used as a restrained brand signature, not a cluttered wordmark stack.
- Footer pattern: small icon on the lower left, page number on the lower right.

## Page Flow

- Cover is one page.
- Lesson roadmap/table of contents is separate.
- Introduction and learning objectives should be compact and, when feasible, fit together.
- Lesson body should flow continuously unless a structural page break improves navigation.
- Summary/key takeaways, glossary, and references should each be clean structural sections.
- Callouts should not appear in structural sections such as references.
- Avoid excessive whitespace and accidental blank areas.

## Callouts

- Callouts are useful only when they add learning value.
- They should not become frequent decorative blocks.
- They should usually be short, often one paragraph, and rarely more than three paragraphs.
- Good callout functions include curiosity, field application, key term, practical example, and bridge/callback.

## Source and Reference Behavior

- User-supplied PDFs/books may guide production, but they are not automatically student-facing references.
- Only published or formally identifiable sources should appear in student-facing references.
- Student references should state the source plainly and should not explain internal source policy.
- Practitioner forums and discussions may inform common confusion and field language, but they are not technical authority unless supported by formal references.
- Each lesson should perform its own source pass even when a course map already contains source guidance.

## Visual Behavior

- Every visual needs a clear learning line.
- Do not include visuals that merely decorate or repeat a previous figure.
- If the image itself carries the core lesson message, prioritize real examples from trustworthy sources instead of generated diagrams.
- For technical symbols, plans, schedules, or real document interpretation, prefer real-source examples or deterministic diagrams based on verified conventions.
- If strong real images are hard to source, create an image curation request with search phrases rather than forcing weak or invented visuals.
- Visual QA must catch failed arrows, bad spacing, blank areas, unreadable text, missing symbols, distorted images, repeated information, and overlaps.
- Text inside visuals should follow a consistent font scale and prioritize legibility over filling space.

## Deck Behavior

- Decks are generated only after explicit human approval of the study guide.
- Decks are presentation-native, not copies of the study guide.
- Default deck: 10 slides for a 10-minute recorded online lesson.
- No speaker notes by default.
- No activities, quizzes, or reflection prompts by default.

## Full-Flow Test Implication

The next full-flow v0 test should evaluate whether these lessons hold up on fresh material. Do not overfit the system to Blueprint Reading only.
